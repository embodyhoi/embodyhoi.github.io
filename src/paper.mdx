## Abstract

Humanoid dexterous manipulation needs reference motions that are both semantically rich and kinematically feasible for the target robot. Text-conditioned hand–object interaction (HOI) generators supply diverse, language-specified demonstrations, but they synthesize motion in human (MANO) space. The embodiment gap is therefore deferred to post-hoc retargeting, where trajectories deployed on the robot can fail to maintain contact with objects and also degrade policy learning. Our key idea is that *robot reachability* should constrain the interaction trajectories *while they are being generated*. We present EmbodyHOI, which runs a differentiable inverse-kinematics (IK) solver for a specific humanoid embodiment inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator. We feed back gradients from the embodiment-aware IK solver into the HOI sampler and jointly optimize the position of the humanoid with respect to the object. Because guidance acts only at inference, one HOI trajectory generator trained on human hand poses transfers across humanoid embodiments without retraining. On ARCTIC trajectories with two contrasting humanoids (Unitree G1 and H1-2), our proposed guidance-based method cuts the retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while leaving text-to-HOI generation quality intact, and raises task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. Critically, cheaper alternatives for bridging the embodiment gap fail to beat EmbodyHOI. Post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection both reach *lower* IK energy than our guidance yet train worse policies.

## Retargeting breaks the interaction it was meant to teach

A text-to-HOI generator produces human hands and object motion; it has never seen a robot. Retargeted as-is to a Unitree G1, the wrists land where the 45 cm arm cannot follow, and the policy trained on that reference reaches only 68% success.

At every flow step the current latent is decoded to wrist poses, a differentiable IK solver on the target humanoid solves for the arm *and* the base, and the gradient of the IK energy is subtracted from the learned velocity field. Only hand tokens are guided; object tokens never change, and the fingers unmask around the corrected wrist over the remaining steps.

$$
\dot{x} \;=\; v_\theta(x, t, z)\; -\; w\,\nabla_{x} E(x), \qquad
E(x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \big\| r\big(u_t^\star(x)\big) \big\|^2,
$$

where $u_t^\star = [q_t; b]$ is the arm-plus-base IK solution for the decoded wrist targets at time $t$, $r(\cdot)$ stacks the pose residual with root-prior, rest-pose, joint-limit, smoothness, clearance and manipulability terms, and gradients flow through the solver by implicit differentiation. The guided samples are retargeted (PyRoki for the arms, dex-retargeting for the fingers) and a PPO policy is trained on them with decoupled arm and hand rewards. Changing the robot changes only the IK module.

## Lower IK energy trains worse policies

If reachability were just a number to minimise, one could refine the finished sample with IK, or draw many samples and keep the most reachable. Both reach *lower* IK energy than EmbodyHOI, and both train worse policies.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unguided, fixed base | 1× | 2.30 | – | 68.1 ± 0.3 |

<Callout>
Post-hoc refinement moves the wrists 33 cm after the fingers and object are already committed. Best-of-32 spends 32× the compute to pick a sample, finds lower energy on 88–94% of prompts, and still loses. Guidance corrects the requested sample; selection re-draws it. Reachability only counts when the rest of the interaction is held fixed.
</Callout>

### Separate arm and hand rewards win on every task

Reaching and finger contact live on different error scales, so the policy imitates the reference with separate arm and hand reward streams. Each component of the recipe contributes:

| Reference → retargeting / RL recipe | Avg. success % |
|---|---|
| ARCTIC ground truth, fixed base → DexMachina-style | 66.8 ± 0.3 |
| Unguided, fixed base → DexMachina-style | 68.2 ± 0.9 |
| Unguided, optimized base → ours | 73.6 ± 0.9 |
| **Guided → ours (full)** | **79.3 ± 0.6** |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;base-placement guidance only | 76.4 ± 0.3 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;arm-conditioning guidance only | 75.3 ± 0.3 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;shared imitation reward | 74.1 ± 0.6 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;shared reward, no contact retargeting | 73.0 ± 0.4 |

## One objective, two robots, opposite base motion

The robot's base translation is a free variable of the same IK solve. From the same start pose, the same objective moves the long-armed H1-2 (68.5 cm arms) *back* from the object to avoid over-extension and the G1 (45 cm arms) *closer* to stay reachable. Neither behaviour is encoded anywhere, and base placement alone cuts near-singular arm poses from 2.6% to 0.2% of samples.

### The same objective carries to a second humanoid

Only the IK module changes; the generator is never retrained. On the H1-2 four-task suite, success rises from 62% to 76%. The same objective also lowers the IK energy of a second generator, Text2HOI, on 48 of 50 prompts for the G1 and on all 50 for the H1-2.
