## Abstract

Humanoid dexterous manipulation needs reference motions that are both semantically rich and kinematically feasible for the target robot. Text-conditioned hand–object interaction (HOI) generators supply diverse, language-specified demonstrations, but they synthesize motion in human (MANO) space. The embodiment gap is therefore deferred to post-hoc retargeting, where trajectories deployed on the robot can fail to maintain contact with objects and also degrade policy learning. Our key idea is that *robot reachability* should constrain the interaction trajectories *while they are being generated*. We present EmbodyHOI, which runs a differentiable inverse-kinematics (IK) solver for a specific humanoid embodiment inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator. We feed back gradients from the embodiment-aware IK solver into the HOI sampler and jointly optimize the position of the humanoid with respect to the object. Because guidance acts only at inference, one HOI trajectory generator trained on human hand poses transfers across humanoid embodiments without retraining. On ARCTIC trajectories with two contrasting humanoids (Unitree G1 and H1-2), our proposed guidance-based method cuts the retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while leaving text-to-HOI generation quality intact, and raises task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. Critically, cheaper alternatives for bridging the embodiment gap fail to beat EmbodyHOI. Post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection both reach *lower* IK energy than our guidance yet train worse policies.

Prior work either trains the generator in robot space, which gives up cross-embodiment reuse, or repairs the motion after generation, when the fingers and the object trajectory are already fixed. EmbodyHOI does neither: it keeps the human-space generator frozen and lets the robot's kinematics steer sampling.

## Method

  <Fragment slot="caption">
    EmbodyHOI makes a frozen HOI generator embodiment-aware at inference. During flow-matching sampling, a differentiable IK module on the target humanoid scores the decoded wrist trajectories and its gradient is added to the learned velocity field.   <div class="rounded-xl bg-zinc-100 p-5">
    <div class="text-xs font-semibold tracking-wide text-rose-700 uppercase">1 · Every flow step</div>
    <p class="mt-2 text-[0.95em] leading-snug text-zinc-800">Decode the wrists from the current latent, solve IK for the arm <em>and</em> the base with Levenberg–Marquardt (10 iterations), and subtract the energy gradient from the velocity field. Only hand tokens are guided; object tokens never change, and the fingers re-form around the corrected wrist over the remaining unmasking steps.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rounded-xl bg-zinc-100 p-5">
    <div class="text-xs font-semibold tracking-wide text-rose-700 uppercase">2 · Base placement in the objective</div>
    <p class="mt-2 text-[0.95em] leading-snug text-zinc-800">The robot's base translation is a free variable of the same IK solve. The per-timestep estimates are averaged into one execution pose that becomes the prior for the next step. This removes near-singular arm configurations (2.6% → 0.2% of samples) at no extra sampling cost.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="rounded-xl bg-zinc-100 p-5">
    <div class="text-xs font-semibold tracking-wide text-rose-700 uppercase">3 · Retarget + RL</div>
    <p class="mt-2 text-[0.95em] leading-snug text-zinc-800">Arms are retargeted with PyRoki, fingers with dex-retargeting, contacts DexMachina-style. A PPO policy (15 upper-body + 24 finger DoF) imitates the reference with <em>separate</em> arm and hand reward streams, because reaching and finger contact live on different error scales.</p>
  </div>

Energy guidance adds a gradient term to the flow of the frozen generator $v_\theta$:

$$
\dot{x} \;=\; v_\theta(x, t, z)\; -\; w\,\nabla_{x} E(x), \qquad
E(x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \big\| r\big(u_t^\star(x)\big) \big\|^2,
$$

where $u_t^\star = [q_t; b]$ is the arm-plus-base IK solution for the decoded wrist targets at time $t$, $r(\cdot)$ stacks the SE(3) pose residual with root-prior, rest-pose, joint-limit, smoothness, clearance and manipulability terms, and gradients flow through the solver by implicit differentiation (JAX, bridged to PyTorch). Guidance costs 32× a native sample (5 s → 160 s on an A100) and is the only extra cost; nothing is retrained.

## Guidance moves the wrist, not the interaction

For matched prompts and seeds, guidance shifts the generated wrists toward configurations the robot can reach (median wrist shift 0.3 cm), while the object trajectory and the grasp structure are preserved. Each clip overlays both generations from the same prompt and seed: the unguided sample has magenta / yellow hands and an orange object, and the IK-guided sample (ours) has green / cyan hands and a blue object. ## Cheaper fixes fail: guidance corrects, selection re-draws

If reachability were just a number to minimise, one could refine the finished sample with IK, or draw many samples and keep the most reachable. Both reach *lower* IK energy than EmbodyHOI, and both train worse policies.

<Figure>
  <Picture slot="figure" src="../assets/figures/compute_vs_success.pdf" alt="Success rate against sampling cost per reference" />
  <Fragment slot="caption">Success rate against sampling cost on the four-task G1 suite. Base placement lifts the unguided baseline for free; best-of-N selection does not measurably improve on it at 32× the cost; IK guidance is the only method that clears the best baseline.</Fragment>
</Figure>

| Method | Cost | IK energy ↓ | Δwrist (cm) | Success % ↑ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unguided, fixed base | 1× | 2.30 | – | 68.1 ± 0.3 |
| + base optimization | 1× | 1.89 | 0 | 73.3 ± 0.5 |
| **+ IK guidance (ours)** | 32× | 1.81 | **0.3** | **79.7 ± 0.4** |
| Post-hoc IK refinement | 1× + IK | **0.35** | 32.6 | 32.8 ± 0.3 |
| Best-of-8 by energy | 8× | 1.36 | 13.2 | 73.1 ± 0.6 |
| Best-of-32 by energy | 32× | 1.21 | 13.7 | 74.0 ± 0.6 |

<Callout>
Post-hoc refinement reaches the lowest energy but moves the wrists 33 cm after the fingers and object are fixed, and success collapses to 33%. Best-of-32 finds lower energy on 88–94% of prompts, but it returns a *different* sample (13 cm away) and trains a worse policy. Guidance corrects the requested sample; selection re-draws it. A feasibility metric is only a valid diagnostic when the rest of the interaction is held fixed.
</Callout>

On 50 random ARCTIC tasks, guided references raise success from 68% to 79%, and from 58% to 82% on the ten hardest tasks (those whose unguided reference is nearly singular). Each component of the recipe contributes:

| Reference → retargeting / RL recipe | Avg. success % |
|---|---|
| ARCTIC ground truth, fixed base → DexMachina-style | 66.8 ± 0.3 |
| Unguided, fixed base → DexMachina-style | 68.2 ± 0.9 |
| Unguided, optimized base → ours | 73.6 ± 0.9 |
| **Guided → ours (full)** | **79.3 ± 0.6** |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;base-placement guidance only | 76.4 ± 0.3 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;arm-conditioning guidance only | 75.3 ± 0.3 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;shared imitation reward | 74.1 ± 0.6 |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;shared reward, no contact retargeting | 73.0 ± 0.4 |

## One objective, two humanoids

Changing the robot changes only the IK module. From the same initial placement, the same objective moves the long-armed H1-2 (178 cm, 68.5 cm arms) *away* from the object to avoid over-extension and the G1 (132 cm, 45 cm arms) *closer and higher* to stay reachable. Neither behaviour is encoded anywhere.

## Failure cases and limitations

<TwoColumns>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    <ClipCard {...deck("g1_mixer_slip_failure")} label="Guidance is kinematic" caption="It makes the grasp reachable, not stable: here the mixer slips during the lift." tag="failure" />
  </Fragment>
  <Fragment slot="right">
    - **Kinematic only.** Guidance reasons about reachability; dynamics and contact stability are left to RL.
    - **32× sampling cost.** 160 s per reference on an A100. Distilling the guidance into the generator is future work.
    - **Fixed lower body.** The base is placed, not walked to; locomotion is not modelled.
    - **Simulation only.** All results are on ARCTIC objects in Genesis; real-robot rollouts are next.
  </Fragment>
</TwoColumns>
