## Abstract

Humanoid dexterous manipulation needs reference motions that are both semantically rich and kinematically feasible for the target robot. Text-conditioned hand–object interaction (HOI) generators supply diverse, language-specified demonstrations, but they synthesize motion in human (MANO) space. The embodiment gap is therefore deferred to post-hoc retargeting, where trajectories deployed on the robot can fail to maintain contact with objects and also degrade policy learning. Our key idea is that *robot reachability* should constrain the interaction trajectories *while they are being generated*. We present EmbodyHOI, which runs a differentiable inverse-kinematics (IK) solver for a specific humanoid embodiment inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator. We feed back gradients from the embodiment-aware IK solver into the HOI sampler and jointly optimize the position of the humanoid with respect to the object. Because guidance acts only at inference, one HOI trajectory generator trained on human hand poses transfers across humanoid embodiments without retraining. On ARCTIC trajectories with two contrasting humanoids (Unitree G1 and H1-2), our proposed guidance-based method cuts the retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while leaving text-to-HOI generation quality intact, and raises task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. Critically, cheaper alternatives for bridging the embodiment gap fail to beat EmbodyHOI. Post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection both reach *lower* IK energy than our guidance yet train worse policies.

## The embodiment gap only surfaces after the interaction is committed

Hand-centric HOI generators are the natural source of dexterous references because they model finger–object contact, but they generate in MANO space with no term that couples the wrist to a robot's kinematic chain. This mismatch only surfaces during retargeting, after the finger and object motions are committed, and the tracking policy inherits wrist targets that the arm cannot reach. Prior work resolves this on either side of generation: training the motion prior in robot space yields one generator per robot, and repairing a generated human motion afterwards only makes delta modifications that cannot always rectify unreachable wrist trajectories.

## Keep the generator frozen, make its samples embodiment-aware during generation

EmbodyHOI keeps a frozen MANO HOI generator (MAD-HOI) as a reusable motion prior and makes its samples embodiment-aware during generation. Inside every ODE step of the flow-matching sampler, we decode the current wrist trajectories, solve a differentiable IK problem on the target humanoid, and add the gradient of the IK residual (the IK energy) to the learned velocity field, in the manner of classifier guidance. A wrist correction made in one unmasking step conditions the finger and object tokens generated in later ones, so the interaction re-forms around the corrected wrist instead of being patched afterward.

**Joint arm and base IK.** We solve for the base because a fixed base makes reachability a property of the trajectory alone: a feasible interaction performed from the wrong standing position is then indistinguishable from an infeasible one, and fixed-base IK rejects both. At each timestep the solve optimizes the arm joints together with a base translation, and because the base is fixed during execution, the per-timestep estimates are averaged into a single execution pose that becomes the root prior for the next unmasking step.

$$
\dot{x} \;=\; v_\theta(x, t, z)\; -\; w\,\nabla_{x} E(x), \qquad
E(x) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \big\| r\big(u_t^\star(x)\big) \big\|^2,
$$

where $u_t^\star = [q_t; b_t]$ is the arm-plus-base solution of a Levenberg–Marquardt solve (10 iterations) for the decoded wrist targets at time $t$, and $r(\cdot)$ stacks the wrist pose residual with a root prior, object clearance, a rest-pose prior, joint-limit, smoothness and manipulability terms. Gradients are taken implicitly through the solver in JAX and bridged to PyTorch; they are zeroed on object tokens, so the generated object trajectory and articulation are bit-identical between a guided sample and its seed-matched unguided sample. All weights are set once and shared by both embodiments, both generators and all tasks. Guidance costs 32× a native sample (5 s to 160 s on an A100), paid once per reference.

On the same 400 ARCTIC test prompts, guided samples have lower FID than unguided (0.545 vs. 0.667), better top-3 retrieval, unchanged contact ratio and diversity, lower penetration and marginally higher KID. Guidance instead removes the residual error that survives retargeting: it lowers the IK energy on 83% of prompts, and the G1's wrist tracking error after retargeting falls from 8.9 cm and 20° to 0.6 cm and 4°.

## IK energy is not the quantity to optimize

Guidance costs 32× a native sample, so each alternative receives the same budget, the same 51 seed-matched prompts, and the same IK solver and base optimizer. Base placement is the cheapest large gain: solving for the base with a single unguided sample reduces the median energy from 2.30 to 1.89 and raises the success rate from 68.1% to 73.3% at no sampling cost. Guidance adds a smaller, consistent correction on top, lowering the energy of the seed-matched sample on 40 of the 51 prompts and raising success to 79.7%.

<Callout title="Guidance corrects, selection re-draws">
Post-hoc refinement reaches the lowest energy of any method (0.35) but moves the wrists by 33 cm after finger and object motion are fixed, and success collapses to 32.8%. Best-of-32 selection lowers the energy further than guidance and wins on 88–94% of prompts, yet its policies succeed less often (74.0% vs. 79.7%, a gap of 5.7 ± 0.7 points at matched compute). The energy measures wrist reachability only; it is blind to whether fingers and object still form a coherent interaction around the wrists it prefers. Energy is a valid diagnostic only at fixed interaction content.
</Callout>

## Guided references raise success from 68% to 79% on 50 random tasks

The 50 tasks are drawn with no selection across all 11 ARCTIC objects, and we train policies from a guided and an unguided reference of each (5 seeds). Success requires the object's position, orientation and articulation to stay within 2 cm, 40° and 10° of the reference at every step of the episode. The gain concentrates where reachability is poor: on the 10 worst-conditioned tasks, selected by the minimum Jacobian singular value of the unguided reference before any policy is trained, success rises from 57.9% to 82.1%.

### Guidance, in-loop base placement and the decoupled reward each raise success

Rows A–D form a ladder on the four-task G1 suite. Swapping the ARCTIC demonstration for an unguided generated sample changes success by 1.4 points, so generation supplies diversity but not reachability. Our RL recipe with post-hoc base optimization adds 5.4 points, and IK guidance with in-loop base placement a further 5.7. Dropping base placement from the full method costs 4.0 points and dropping the arm-conditioning terms 2.9; re-solving the base inside the sampling loop rather than once after generation accounts for 2.8 of the 5.7 points. A shared imitation reward costs 5.2 points.

## The same generator and IK objective place two humanoids differently

Shifting to a new robot only needs a change in the IK module. From the same initial placement, guidance moves the H1-2 base (178 cm tall, 68.5 cm arms) away from the object to avoid over-extension and the G1 base (132 cm, 45 cm arms) closer and higher to stay reachable. No term in the objective encodes either behaviour; both fall out of the same residual. On the G1, base placement alone cuts the share of near-singular samples from 2.6% to 0.2%.

### The gain carries through to policy learning on the H1-2

On the H1-2, guidance lowers the IK energy of the seed-matched sample on 46 of 50 prompts while moving the wrists by 0.3 cm, and raises success on the four-task suite from 62.3% to 75.7%, a 13.4-point gain comparable to the G1's 11.6 points despite the different arm geometry. The module is also generator-agnostic: applied without modification to Text2HOI's DDPM sampler, it lowers the IK energy on 48 of 50 ARCTIC prompts on the G1 and on all 50 on the H1-2.

## Guidance is kinematic

<TwoColumns>
  <Fragment slot="left">
    <ClipCard {...deck("g1_mixer_slip_failure")} label="Failure case" caption="The mixer slips from the right hand during the lift. Better reachability does not imply dynamic or contact feasibility; the RL stage absorbs what guidance does not model." tag="failure" />

  <Fragment slot="right">
    - **Kinematic only.** Guidance does not reason about dynamics or contact. Reachable references are nonetheless closer to executable ones: guided references have a lower median peak-velocity ratio against the G1's joint limits than their unguided counterparts (0.20 vs. 0.24).
    - **32× slower sampler.** Paid once per reference and amortized over about 4 h of policy training; restricting guidance to a subset of the unmasking steps would reduce it.
    - **Fixed lower body.** We optimize where the robot stands, not how it gets there; integrating a locomotion controller is future work.
    - **Simulation only.** All evaluation is in simulation on ARCTIC's articulated objects; real-world rollouts are the immediate next step.
